# CLIO — Base Rate Library

CLIO's "institutional memory." Each entry is a reference class with a historical frequency.
Figures marked *(approx.)* are from general historical knowledge and should be verified against
a primary source when a question depends heavily on them; when verified, replace *(approx.)*
with the source. Add new entries whenever a new kind of question appears.

## Politics & elections

### Midterm elections
- **President's party loses House seats in midterms:** 18 of 20 midterms, 1946–2022.
  Exceptions: 1998 (Clinton), 2002 (Bush, post-9/11). Base rate ≈ **90%**.
- **Average House loss for president's party:** ≈ 26 seats since 1946 *(approx.)*.
  When presidential approval is **below 50%**, the average loss is ≈ 37 seats *(approx., Gallup)*.
- **President's party loses Senate seats:** roughly 14 of 20 midterms since 1946 *(approx.)*.
  Gains in 1962, 1970, 2002, 2018, 2022. The Senate depends far more on **which seats are up** (the map)
  than the House does.
- **Wave years where a party gained ≥ 4 Senate seats:** 1958, 1980 (presidential year), 1986, 1994, 2006 (+6 D),
  2014 (+9 R) *(approx.)*. Net +4 is achievable but needs a strong national environment.
- **Presidential approval at the midterm vs. outcome:** No president below ~45% approval has seen
  his party gain House seats in a midterm *(approx.)*.
- **Generic ballot:** Final generic-ballot polling average has generally pointed in the right direction for the
  House popular vote; average miss ≈ 2–4 pts *(approx.)*.

### Incumbency
- **House incumbents seeking re-election win:** ≈ 90–98% in most cycles *(approx.)*. Lower in wave years.
- **Senate incumbents seeking re-election win:** ≈ 80–90% *(approx.)*; lower in wave years for the
  disadvantaged party.
- **Polling vs. incumbents:** Senate polling averages have missed by ~4–5 pts on average in recent cycles,
  and sometimes systematically (e.g., Maine 2020: Collins trailed in nearly every poll and won by ~8–9).
  Treat a lead under ~4 pts in a polling average as close to a toss-up.

### State-level patterns
- **Texas:** No Democrat has won a statewide race since 1994. Strong structural Republican lean.
- **Ohio:** Trended Republican since 2016; Brown lost his seat in 2024 by ~3.5 pts.
- **Alaska:** Republican at federal level; ranked-choice voting; Peltola won House races in 2022 but lost in 2024.
- **Maine:** Collins has won 5 Senate races, often outrunning polls.
- **Iowa:** Trended strongly Republican since 2016.

### Turnout (voting-eligible population, US Elections Project)
- Midterms: 2010 ≈ 41.8%, 2014 ≈ 36.7%, 2018 ≈ 50.0% (highest since 1914), 2022 ≈ 46.6% *(approx.)*.
- High-intensity, polarized environments raise midterm turnout.

### Congress & government funding
- **Shutdowns:** Historically rare, but recent era is far more volatile — FY2026 began with the
  longest shutdown in US history, followed by two partial shutdowns. Lame-duck sessions (after
  an election, before new Congress) usually pass a stopgap or omnibus, but a party that just won
  big may prefer to wait for the new Congress.

## Economy & markets

### Federal Reserve
- The Fed usually moves in a **series** once it starts a new direction, but data-dependent chairs often
  pause between moves *(general pattern)*.
- The Fed has historically avoided appearing political near elections, but **has** moved rates in
  pre-election meetings when data demanded *(approx.)*.
- A hike while job growth is weak is historically uncommon; it signals the Fed prioritizing inflation.

### Inflation & data releases
- Monthly CPI headline swings are dominated by **energy** (gasoline). Core is stickier.
- Energy shocks pass into headline CPI within 1–2 months; into core more slowly.
- **Producer prices amplify energy moves** (learned 2026-10-16): Sept 2026 gasoline was +9.1% in CPI
  but +20.3% in PPI, and PPI final demand jumped 1.3% m/m (6.3% y/y). When CPI energy jumps 5%+ in a
  month, a 0.5%+ PPI headline is the likely outcome, not the unusual one.
- **Markets look through energy-led PPI:** Oct 28 hike odds stayed ~27–34% after the 1.3% PPI print
  (CME FedWatch), so a hot PPI alone is weak evidence of a Fed move.
- Monthly jobs reports: first prints are noisy (± ~100k typical revision range historically *(approx.)*).

### Growth & recession
- Post-WWII US has had ~12 recessions; on average about one every 6–7 years *(approx.)*.
- **Oil-price shocks preceded several recessions** (1973, 1979–80, 1990, 2008).
- Two consecutive negative GDP quarters is rare outside recessions.
- Advance GDP estimates are revised, sometimes by more than 1 pt.

### Stock market
- S&P 500 rises on ~53% of trading days and ~60–63% of months *(approx.)*.
- Historically, the S&P 500 has been higher 12 months after every midterm election since 1950 *(approx.)*.
- Oil spikes, rate hikes and stagflation fears historically compress equity valuations.

### Oil
- Prices driven by supply disruptions mean-revert once the disruption ends, but a disruption
  of the Strait of Hormuz (≈ 20% of world oil flows *(approx.)*) is among the most severe possible.

## Unrest & social

- **Mass peaceful protest:** Large coordinated US protest days (Women's March 2017, No Kings 2025)
  have been overwhelmingly peaceful; arrests and injuries are low relative to size.
- Organizer-reported crowd totals tend to exceed independent estimates.
- **Federal troop deployments** to US cities over protest are historically rare but occurred in
  2020 and 2025 (Los Angeles); courts have repeatedly constrained Guard deployments (Portland 2025).
- Political violence spikes historically cluster around elections and high-polarization events.

## Foreign affairs

- **Interstate wars:** Most last longer than initially expected; ceasefire announcements frequently
  break down within weeks *(general pattern)*.
- **Tanker wars:** The 1980s Iran–Iraq "Tanker War" ran ~1984–1988 with hundreds of ships attacked;
  shipping attacks tend to recur weekly once a campaign is underway.
- **Russia–Ukraine:** Every announced short truce since 2022 has been violated or lapsed; no
  durable general ceasefire through mid-2026.
- **US strikes on Houthis (Yemen):** Occurred repeatedly 2024–2025 when Red Sea shipping was attacked.
